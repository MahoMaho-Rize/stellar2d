                                                                                                        ABSTRACT
                                                         Core-collapse supernovae (CCSNe) are among the primary sources of dust in galaxies. In this
                                                       study, we derive theoretical upper limits on dust masses as a function of supernova (SN) progenitors
arXiv:2508.12933v1 [astro-ph.SR] 18 Aug 2025




                                                       with initial masses between 9 and 120 M⊙ , based on previously established models of dust formation
                                                       chemistry in CCSNe. We find that O-rich dust, particularly silicates, dominates the dust budget, with
                                                       masses ranging from 0.02 to 0.9 M⊙ , and that the total mass of O-rich dust increases with progenitor
                                                       mass. C-rich amorphous carbon dust is significant for lower-mass progenitors (up to 15 M⊙ ), but its
                                                       mass never exceeds 0.05 M⊙ . For progenitors up to 30 M⊙ , we provide best-fit functions describing
                                                       the masses of O-rich dust, C-rich dust, and CO molecules. A large stochastic variation is found in the
                                                       predicted masses of silicate dust, which correlates with the randomness of shell-merger events in the
                                                       pre-explosion phases of massive stars. Furthermore, we show that the dust mass for a given progenitor
                                                       can vary by a factor of 2–5, reflecting differences in pre-explosion abundance profiles predicted by
                                                       the stellar evolution codes KEPLER and MESA. We emphasize that the final dust yield in CCSNe is
                                                       primarily determined by stochastic stellar yields and uncertainties in pre-explosion nucleosynthesis,
                                                       while explosion properties mainly influence the timescales of dust formation.
